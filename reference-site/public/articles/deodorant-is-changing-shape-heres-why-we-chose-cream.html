Deodorant Is Changing Shape. Here's Why We Chose Cream.

For decades, deodorant came in two familiar forms: a stick or a spray. Walk into a major retailer now and the shelf looks very different.

Gels. Creams. Mists. Whole-body deodorants. Products made for feet, thighs, skin folds and other high-sweat areas, not just underarms.

Deodorant hasn't suddenly forgotten what it is. It's finally catching up with how people actually use it.

Why the Category Is Expanding

Traditional antiperspirants were built around one primary job: reducing underarm sweat. Their format followed that purpose. A solid stick delivered aluminum compounds directly to the underarm, while aerosols sprayed them over a wider area.

The growth of aluminum-free and whole-body deodorants has widened the category beyond traditional sweat prevention. When the goal is controlling odour rather than reducing perspiration, brands have more freedom to rethink where deodorant is used and what form it takes.

Odour doesn't happen only under your arms. Neither does friction, moisture or skin sensitivity. Creams and gels can be applied precisely to different external areas, while sprays make it easier to cover larger ones.

Consumers are also paying closer attention to ingredients and skin comfort. They want products that feel more like skincare: easy to spread, gentle on sensitive areas and available in unscented or baking-soda-free options.

The result is more choice, but also more confusion. A product labelled “whole body” may be a cream, spray or stick, and each format behaves differently. Format is no longer just a packaging decision. It affects where the product can go, how much you use and what has to be added to make it work.

Why BPM Is a Cream

We chose a cream because it gives you control.

A small amount can be spread thinly and widely, exactly where it's needed. There's no rigid stick dragging across the skin, no propellant carrying product into the air and no need to force the formula into a shape it doesn't naturally want to hold.

That last part mattered to us.

Producing a conventional solid stick would have required additional structuring ingredients, typically waxes or similar materials, whose main job would be keeping the product firm inside its container. We didn't want to add ingredients simply to satisfy a familiar format.

Instead, we built the formula around performance. Magnesium hydroxide and zinc ricinoleate address odour. Tapioca starch and kaolin help manage moisture. Lightweight oils give the cream glide and allow it to spread evenly.

Every ingredient has a job.

The cream works for underarms, but it isn't trapped there. It can also be applied to appropriate external high-sweat areas where a conventional stick would be awkward or imprecise. You decide where it goes and how much you need.

It is also concentrated. Start with a pea-sized amount, spread it thinly and let the formula do the work. More product doesn't mean more protection.

The Packaging Shaped the Format Too

We considered paperboard packaging, but it didn't meet our standard for durability. Deodorant lives in humid bathrooms, gets handled with damp hands and gets crushed inside gym bags and luggage. Paper can soften, ripple and lose its shape in exactly those conditions.

We explain that decision more fully in What's Changing in Deodorant Packaging Right Now (and Why We Went With Metal).

Metal gave us the durability and recyclability we wanted without requiring a complicated plastic dispensing mechanism. A collapsible tube also works naturally with a cream formula.

That connection matters: we didn't develop a formula and then find something to put it in. The formula, format and packaging were designed to work together.

Choosing cream meant we could use a durable metal tube. Choosing the tube meant we didn't have to add extra ingredients just to create a solid stick.

The Stick Isn't Disappearing

Sticks and sprays will remain popular because they're familiar and convenient. What's changing is the assumption that they're the only forms deodorant should take.

The category is moving away from one product, one body part and one method of application. That means more useful options for different bodies, routines and skin types.

BPM chose cream because it gave us the most direct route to the product we wanted to make: concentrated, adaptable, wax-free and packaged to withstand real life.
